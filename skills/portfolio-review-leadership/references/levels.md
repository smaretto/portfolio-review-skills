# Leadership levels and archetypes

## Effective levels

| Effective level | Common titles | Typical scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manager** | Design Manager, Head of Design at small companies, Lead with reports | 3 to 8 direct reports, mostly ICs; one product area or a few squads |
| **Senior Manager** | Senior Design Manager, Head of Design at growing companies | 6 to 12 reports, may include a lead; several teams in a domain |
| **Director** | Director of Design, Head of Design at scale-ups, Associate Director at large companies | Manager of managers or leads; roughly 15 to 50 designers; several domains or a product line |
| **Senior Director** | Senior Director, Head of Design at larger scale-ups | Several directors or a large function, roughly 40 to 100+; company-wide practice |

Numbers are typical ranges, not thresholds. Use the combination of signals
below.

**Below Manager:** a Lead or Staff designer with one or two reports, or
informal leadership only, is a player-coach. If the portfolio reads this way,
say so, review it against Manager with that caveat, and mention that the IC
skill may give a better read.

**Above Senior Director:** if the portfolio reads as VP or executive, say it
is beyond this skill's calibration and review it against Senior Director.

## Scope signals

| Signal | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Direct reports | Number and roles (ICs, leads, managers) |
| Indirect reports | Total org size, only when reporting lines are stated |
| Managers of managers | Whether managers or leads report to them. The clearest Director signal. |
| Breadth | Number of teams, domains or products; disciplines covered (product design, research, content, brand, design ops) |
| Reporting line | To whom they reported (manager, director, VP, CPO, CEO) |
| Decision rights | Headcount, budget, hiring decisions, org structure, ladder ownership |
| Exec exposure | Leadership team membership, board or C-level work |
| Company stage and size | Startup, scale-up, enterprise; size of product and engineering org |
| Leadership through senior ICs | Staff, lead or principal ICs acting as delegates for coaching, delivery or quality. Partial layering evidence when the portfolio says what was delegated, how, and with what result. Not equivalent to managers reporting. |
| Managers in development | A report being developed into a management role, described with the plan and where it stood. Counts toward layering and People Development. |

### Title inflation and deflation

- A "Head of Design" with 2 to 5 ICs and no managers reads as **Manager**,
  however senior the reporting line. Report it as "title: Head of Design;
  effective level: Manager".
- The only design leader at a startup, reporting to the CEO, often has
  executive exposure beyond their org size. Record that breadth as a
  distinctive signal. It does not make them a Director of a multi-level org.
- At large companies, titles can be deflated (an Associate Director or
  Senior Manager running a large org). Read scope, not title, in both
  directions.
- VP titles at small companies (roughly under 300 people, design team under
  about 20) often carry Director scope. Review them against Director or
  Senior Director by scope. True VP scope (several layers, function-wide
  budget, executive team membership) stays out of scope for this skill.
- If reporting lines are not stated, the level read is low confidence. Say
  so and make it an interview question.

## Core competencies per level

Used by the verdict caps in verdicts-and-calibration.md.

| Effective level | Core |
| --- | --- |
| Manager | Team Building & Org Design, People Development, Quality Bar & Craft Leadership, Cross-functional & Executive Partnership |
| Senior Manager | Manager core plus Operating Model |
| Director | Team Building & Org Design, People Development, Operating Model, Design & Product Strategy, Cross-functional & Executive Partnership, Business Impact |
| Senior Director | Director core plus Leading Change |

Quality Bar & Craft Leadership stays important at Director and above but is
not core, because some strong directors lead through managers who hold the
bar. If the target role or JD stresses hands-on craft, make it core.

## Leadership portfolio shape

A leadership portfolio should show the organisation, not only the work.
Expect at least some of:

- before and after accounts of a team or practice
- team structure, size and reporting lines
- hiring, ladder or development artifacts or specific descriptions
- strategy, principles or operating model with their effect
- business outcomes with the leader's mechanism
- selected team work, credited to the team, to show the quality bar

Common shapes to name in the report:

- **IC portfolio with a leadership title**: mostly the candidate's own
  screens. Reads as IC. Serious red flag for Director targets, Watch for
  Manager targets.
- **Org portfolio with no product**: org charts and rituals, no sign of the
  work or its outcomes. Weak on Quality Bar and Business Impact.
- **Balanced**: organisation, practice and product outcomes linked. The
  target shape.

## Archetypes

Identify which archetype or archetypes the portfolio presents. They describe
the kind of leader, and shape where a company would get most from them.

| Archetype | Expected spikes | Fits | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Builder** (founding or first design leader) | Team Building, Leading Change, Partnership | Early-stage, first design hire, function from scratch | Has not yet led managers or a mature org |
| **Scaler** | Org Design, Operating Model, People Development | Fast-growing scale-ups | Process heavy where speed matters |
| **Rebuilder / turnaround** | Leading Change, Operating Model, Quality Bar | Teams that lost direction, quality or trust | Short tenures without durable results shown |
| **Craft leader / player-coach** | Quality Bar, Strategy | Craft-led products, small senior teams | Limited org-building evidence |
| **Operator** | Operating Model, systems governance | Large or complex orgs, multi-product | Little product or strategy influence |
| **Product-leaning leader** | Strategy, Business Impact, Partnership | Product-led companies, design-and-product hybrid roles | Weaker people development or craft evidence |
| **AI transformation leader** | Operating Model (AI workflows), Strategy (AI products), Quality Bar | Companies moving product and practice to AI | AI adoption claimed as output volume rather than better decisions |
