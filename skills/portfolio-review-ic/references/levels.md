# IC levels and archetypes

## Canonical ladder

| Canonical | Common titles | Approx. US tech mapping | Typical years (weak signal) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Junior | Junior / Associate / Designer I | Google L3, Meta IC3 | 0 to 2 |
| Mid | Product Designer / Designer II | Google L4, Meta IC4 | 2 to 5 |
| Senior | Senior Product Designer | Google L5, Meta IC5 | 5 to 8 |
| Staff | Staff / Lead / Senior II | Google L6, Meta IC6 | 8+ |
| Senior Staff | Senior Staff / Lead | Google L7, Meta IC7 | 10+ |
| Principal | Principal | Google L7 to L8, Meta IC7 to IC8 | 12+ |

Notes:

- Years are the weakest signal. Use scope first.
- European and smaller companies often use a flatter ladder (Junior, Mid,
  Senior, Lead, Principal). Map to the canonical rows by scope.
- "Lead Designer" is ambiguous: an IC at Staff level, a senior IC with
  informal leadership, or a player-coach with reports. Read the scope. If it
  includes direct reports and people management, the leadership skill may be
  more appropriate; say so.
- If the user names a company ladder (for example "L5 at a large US tech
  company", "IC6"), use the mapping above and state it is approximate.

## Scope signals

Use these to decide what level the portfolio reads as.

| Signal | Junior | Mid | Senior | Staff | Principal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem scope | Tasks and screens | Features | A product area or major flow | Several areas or a cross-cutting problem | Product line or company-level problem |
| Ambiguity | Well-defined briefs | Defined problems, open solutions | Ill-defined problems; frames them | Defines which problems matter | Sets direction where none exists |
| Autonomy | Guided | Mostly independent | Independent; guides others | Self-directing; others rely on them | Sets agendas for others |
| Impact radius | Own work | Own feature and team | Team and partners | Several teams | Organisation |
| Time horizon | Weeks | Weeks to a quarter | Quarters | Half-year to a year | Years |

Read the level from the **highest scope consistently shown**, not the single
most impressive claim. One Staff-scope project among Mid-scope ones reads as
Mid with a Staff-level moment, worth probing.

## Core competencies per level

These are the competencies used by the verdict caps in
verdicts-and-calibration.md. Non-core competencies still get rated and still
matter for the profile shape, but a weakness there does not cap the verdict.

| Level | Core |
| --- | --- |
| Junior | Craft & Taste, UX & Research, Ownership |
| Mid | Craft & Taste, UX & Research, Product Judgment, Ownership |
| Senior | Craft & Taste, UX & Research, Product Judgment, Ownership, Influence, Building |
| Staff, Senior Staff | Product Judgment, Influence, Ownership, Systems Thinking, plus at least one area of depth rated Above |
| Principal | Product Judgment, Influence, Ownership, plus a clear spike (Above) that defines their contribution |

<!--
  Building is core from Senior because coded and AI-built prototyping is now
  baseline practice for product designers. For Craft-led and Research-led
  archetypes it can be downgraded to non-core; see archetypes below. Revisit
  this when practice moves again.
-->

AI as Material becomes core at any level when the target role or JD is for
an AI product.

For junior and mid candidates, potential matters: rate growth signals
(learning shown across projects, self-initiated work, reflection on what they
would do differently) in the profile section, even though they are not a
competency.

## Archetypes

Product designers first. Identify the archetype the portfolio presents. It
shapes the expected spikes and how weaknesses are weighed. A designer can
match two.

| Archetype | Expected spikes | Acceptable lighter areas | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Generalist product designer** | Balanced across UX & Research, Product Judgment, Craft | None strongly below level | Breadth with no depth anywhere |
| **Craft-led product designer** | Craft & Taste, Systems Thinking | Building can be non-core | Polish without decisions or outcomes |
| **Builder / design engineer** | Building, Systems Thinking, often AI as Material | Research can be lighter if testing in code replaces it | Output without user evidence; code as the story instead of decisions |
| **AI product designer** | AI as Material, Building, Product Judgment | Visual craft can be lighter | Happy-path AI only; tool use mistaken for AI product design |
| **Research-led product designer** | UX & Research, Product Judgment | Building can be non-core | Insights that never reach shipped design |
| **Systems specialist** | Systems Thinking, Craft, Influence | Product Judgment on single features | System work with no adoption or effect shown |

## Out of scope

The IC skill is calibrated for product designers. If the portfolio is mainly
brand, marketing, illustration, motion, pure UX research or content design:

- say so in the Inputs section
- still give a portfolio effectiveness read and a profile read
- give the verdict with an explicit caveat that the competency model does not
  fit the discipline, and name which competencies were not meaningful
